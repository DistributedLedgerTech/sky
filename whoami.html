PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTUREEST. 2020

How real satellite networks circle the planet: 48 satellites in six near-polar rings, modeled on Iridium, about 780 km up, each lapping Earth roughly every 100 minutes. Each keeps laser links to the satellites ahead, behind and in the next ring, so data hops around the world without touching the ground; near the poles those side links let go, and where two rings pass in opposite directions (the seam) they never link. Each city talks to the highest satellite in its sky and hands off as it sets. The three on the outer ring are geostationary, 35,786 km up, parked over fixed points on the equator. Altitudes compressed and motion sped up to fit the screen.

[01] CONVICTION

I do not wait for the future. I build it.

I architected a post-quantum monetary system and then built its chain, miners, wallet, explorer, and encrypted communications. The work is shipped and open to inspection.

My responsibility is simple: say exactly what I mean, make the work verifiable, and keep building until powerful technology is useful to ordinary people. Every word I publish, I stand behind. Every rule, anyone can read.

[02] MY FOUNDATION

Core values are house rules.

I wrote these standards for myself first. They govern what I build, how I speak, who I serve, and what I refuse to compromise.

CORE VALUE / 01

Integrity

I keep the record straight. Claims should resolve to running code, public evidence, or a clear admission that the work is not finished yet. My word and my work must agree—even when honesty is less convenient than hype.

“If I cannot verify it, I do not ask you to believe it.”

CORE VALUE / 02

Service before self

I build to transfer capability, not collect dependency. The point of a wallet is user control. The point of an explorer is public truth. The point of leadership is to carry the difficult part until others are strong enough to carry it too.

“The system succeeds when more people can stand on their own.”

CORE VALUE / 03

Excellence in all I do

I treat every line of code, every interface, every support answer, and every promise as part of the same standard. Excellence is not spectacle. It is disciplined follow-through, repeated long after the announcement is over.

“Build it carefully. Operate it honestly. Improve it relentlessly.”

[03] VALUES IN PRACTICE

The standard has to show up in the product.

Principles only matter when they change decisions. These are the operating tests I apply across DLT.

01

Can people verify it?

Network claims should lead to an explorer, documentation, source, or visible operation.

02

Does it increase agency?

A product should help users understand, control, create, or participate—not merely consume.

03

Does it work outside the pitch?

The interface and infrastructure must make sense when no one is standing nearby to explain them.

04

Can the next person grow?

Documentation, internships, and community knowledge should make the ecosystem less dependent on one mind over time.
